A plain-language tour · July 2026

A folder that works like a
small software studio

AgentBox is a single folder on a computer where AI workers propose the next job, do the job, check each other's work, and publish it — while a human keeps the veto and sets the taste.

Last night, mostly unsupervised, it designed the rulebook for a card game, wrote sixty-plus cards, illustrated them, and put the whole thing on a website.

Before we built anything

We did the homework: how does the industry
run AI-driven development?

A research survey of the field — centered on Amazon's AI-DLC method ("AI-Driven Development Life Cycle") and the projects around it — found the same three ingredients everywhere:

  • Start from a goal, not a to-do list. A human states an intent; the AI breaks it into small, finishable pieces.
  • A small cast of specialist roles. Not one giant brain — a planner, a builder, a checker, each with one job.
  • A paper trail with human checkpoints. Every step writes files a person can read, and risky moments pause for approval.
Standing on shoulders

What we borrowed, and from whom

AWS AI-DLC · awslabs/aidlc-workflows

The skeleton and the vocabulary: big goals become small units of work, built in short bursts called bolts, and every step leaves its plan and evidence on disk where anyone can read it.

CrewAI · multi-agent role casting

The idea of a small named cast instead of one do-everything AI. Ours is five: an idea-maker, a planner, an exam-writer, a builder, and an inspector — each forbidden from doing the others' jobs.

Devin & Cursor · the hard lessons

Give each job its own isolated copy of the project so workers can't trample each other; be extra-paranoid near money and security; and never take the worker's word that the work is good.

Human-in-the-loop patterns · incl. Wiz & FIRE

Approval gates at the risky moments — and our twist, the veto window: routine proposals go to the human's phone, and polite silence after fair warning counts as a yes. The pipeline never waits on anyone's energy.

The paranoia, on purpose

When a machine says its own work is good —
who checks, besides the machine?

  • The exam exists before the work — and a script confirms it fails first, so a fake exam can't sneak in.
  • The inspector never talks to the builder. It sees only the assignment and the finished result — and a first-try pass triggers a second, deliberately hostile inspection.
  • Some requirements are kept secret from the builder entirely, checked only at the end. You can't study for a test you've never seen.
  • "Done" is a receipt, not a claim — computed by a simple script from facts no AI can author. And anything touching money or real accounts always waits for the human.

The bottom line

A zip file that is a software studio

Copy the folder to any computer, add one AI login, and it wakes up knowing its job — its instructions, its diary, and the ledger of everything the human ever taught it travel inside the folder.

The human supplies taste and judgment. The box supplies the hours. And the answer to "who checks, besides the machine?" is: the exam, the stranger, the secret question, and the receipt — every single time.
